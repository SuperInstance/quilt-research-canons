The doctrine, stated as a design constraint and then built as an instrument.

## The claim

A system can win at looking like it is searching while producing the same thing every
time. The failure is not dishonesty — it is that **convergence is the objective function**.
Any objective that rewards proximity to a target produces proximity to a target, in as many
costumes as you let it dress up in.

The correction:

> **Many routes to the same answer make systems durable. We are not looking for the best.
> We are looking for what is preferred when.**

Preferred-when is a question with a different shape, because the answer is a function of
state rather than a ranking. The best is a point on a line; preferred is a region of a
space, and the region moves.

## What the fleet already does that this names

- **12 language ports of one cell model.** Not primarily portability. The twelfth port is
  the one that finds out the first eleven agreed for reasons unrelated to the model. When
  a port breaks and the kernel does not, you learn where the kernel is actually
  load-bearing — and no single-route system could have produced that finding.
- **Two ecosystems kept deliberately separate** (polyformalism C99, and the typed runtimes).
  They are two routes that both say something true about a cell. The distance between them
  is where the reading lives.

## The instrument

Four independent implementations of one semantic — a double-entry book that must balance.
The report is not whether they agree. It is **where they disagree**.

| case | t-account | graph-walk | involution | categorical | distinct |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| balanced | yes | yes | yes | yes | 1/4 |
| unbalanced by 2 | **no** | **no** | **no** | **yes** | **2/4** |
| same sign | no | no | no | no | 1/4 |

On a clean book all four agree, which is correct — a real invariant holds on every route.
On an unbalanced book the R-valued routes reject while the categorical one accepts, because
balance in Z/2 is a strictly weaker claim than balance in R.

**That disagreement is not a bug. It measures the strength of the invariant, and a single
route could never have told you** — it has nothing to disagree with.

A route that never disagrees carries no information. A route that disagrees constantly
carries noise. The preference function has to sit between them.

## The open problem, named

**What makes a fifth route worth adding?** Not a score. The current dial is set by whether
a disagreement turned out to be about something real, which is a retrospective judgement and
therefore a weak instrument. That is the part still being worked out, and it is stated in
the essay rather than papered over.

## The self-critique

I have built this by accident at least once. A loop that demanded novelty every round and
scored every proposal converged anyway, because scoring is a funnel and funnels have one
bottom. Forty rounds of fresh vocabulary, one idea in the artifact, and a transcript that
reads like discovery.

Naming it is the useful part: **novelty in process, sameness in product**. It is invisible
from inside, because the loop reports successes and the artifact has one thing in it, and
both statements are true. You need both to notice.
