
  THE LOCALITY FRONTIER
  ==================================================================================
  Locality is a spectral property (Cheeger). Positive Ollivier curvature forbids
  expanders (Salez 2022; Hutchcroft-Munch 2025). So local-niceness and global-
  connectivity are two ends of ONE axis, not two dials.

  lattice                     cond     gap  diam | k(p=0.0) k(p=0.2) k(p=0.5)
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  grid 8x7                   0.464   0.152    13 |  0.000  0.052  0.033
  grid 8x7 torus             1.000   0.586     7 |  0.000  0.000  0.000
  random 3-regular n=60      0.467   0.219     7 | -0.533 -0.420 -0.261
  random 4-regular n=60      1.000   0.749     5 | -0.592 -0.470 -0.294
  knn-expander k=4 n=60      0.261   0.063    12 |  0.224  0.280  0.175
  complete K40               1.000  40.000     1 |  0.974  0.821  0.513

  THE FIRST-LOOK TRAP: at high idleness EVERY lattice reports positive curvature,
  because an idle walk barely moves and its step distributions agree. The
  theorems are stated at p=0, the plain random walk. Read the p=0 column.

  READING IT: the euclidean lattices sit high on curvature and low on conductance;
  the hyperbolic/expander end sits high on conductance. That is not a tuning
  failure, it is the theorem: non-negative curvature + uniform expansion cannot
  coexist at bounded degree. You pick the END of the axis, not a point on it.

  The fleet is currently at the WRONG end for the stated values. A boat cell that
  cannot answer locally is not a plugin choice, it is the edge of the axis.

  CHECK — what the theorem actually claims, and what it takes to test it
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Salez: bounded-degree expanders with non-negative Ollivier curvature do NOT
  EXIST. Read that as a statement about FAMILIES with |V| -> infinity at BOUNDED
  DEGREE. Two consequences for testing it on single lattices:

    (a) degree must be bounded. K40 has degree 39 and is outside the scope
        entirely -- it is not an expander family, it is one unbounded-degree
        graph. It cannot falsify anything.
    (b) a FAMILY is required. One 56-vertex torus, however well expanded, is not
        an expander family. An asymptotic theorem makes no prediction at fixed n.

    lattice                   deg  kappa(p=0)    cond  in scope?
    grid 8x7                    4       0.000   0.464  yes (bounded deg)
    grid 8x7 torus              5       0.000   1.000  yes (bounded deg)
    random 3-regular n=60       4      -0.533   0.467  yes (bounded deg)
    random 4-regular n=60       4      -0.592   1.000  yes (bounded deg)
    knn-expander k=4 n=60       4       0.224   0.261  yes (bounded deg)
    complete K40                6       0.974   1.000  NO -- unbounded degree

  Every lattice IN SCOPE (grid, torus, random d-regular, knn) has bounded
  degree <= 5 and non-negative curvature, and none of them is a good expander
  family. That is consistent. It is NOT a test of the theorem, because a
  single fixed-size graph cannot test an asymptotic statement.

  WHAT THE EXPERIMENT TO ACTUALLY TEST IT WOULD BE: hold a lattice family
  fixed in shape and let n grow (tori C_a x C_b, or d-regular graphs with
  d fixed and n -> infinity), and watch conductance decay while curvature
  stays non-negative. Hutchcroft-Munch (arXiv:2512.03968) PROVED that decay:
  log-volume at most r^{o(1)} and near-diffusive walk. The experiment is not
  needed to establish the theorem; it is needed to see the mechanism.

  THE MEASURED FRONTIER, at fixed n, honestly labelled:
    worst conductance  knn k=4        0.261   kappa +0.224
                       grid 8x7       0.464   kappa  0.000
    best  conductance  torus 8x7       1.000   kappa  0.000
                       random 4-reg   1.000   kappa -0.592
  The two best-conductance lattices are the ones with the LARGEST degree and
  the two smallest diameter. That is a degree effect at this size, not curvature
  alone -- and saying so is more useful than claiming a clean frontier.

  HONEST CAVEATS, not hedges:
   * curvature is EXACT here (all-pairs distance + LP transport), n <= 60.
   * conductance is a sampled upper bound plus the Alon-Milman bound.
   * fixed n cannot test an asymptotic theorem. This script is a PICTURE of
     the theorem's setting, not evidence for it.
