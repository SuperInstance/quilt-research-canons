  THE RESULT, WHICH CONTRADICTS THE STORY I WROTE BEFORE RUNNING IT
  ==============================================================================
  I drafted the conclusion above the data and guessed wrong. Here is what the
  numbers actually say.

      TORUS  C_a x C_b   kappa = 0.000 exactly   cond 1.000 -> 0.612  DECAYS
      GRID   a x b       kappa = 0.000 exactly   cond 0.500 -> 0.319  DECAYS
      RANDOM 3-regular   kappa < 0              cond 0.500 -> 0.603  FLAT
      RANDOM 4-regular   kappa < 0              cond 0.800 -> 1.000  FLAT/GROWS

  So the non-negatively curved (flat) families LOSE their expansion as they
  grow, and the negatively curved random ones KEEP it. That is Salez's theorem
  confirmed on both sides, which is the opposite of what I predicted.

  AND MY INSTRUMENT UNDERSTATES THE DECAY BY A LOT. There is an exact answer
  for the torus:

      h(C_n x C_n) = 4/n        (attained by a half-torus slab)
      lambda_2    ~ 4*pi^2/n^2

  At n=196 the TRUE conductance is 4/196 = 0.0204. My table says 0.612 -- a
  30x-looser UPPER BOUND. The 'cond' column above n=24 is a sampled ball-grow
  estimate OR'd with the Alon-Milman bound, and the Alon-Milman bound
  sqrt(2*d*lambda_2) decays like 1/sqrt(n) while the truth decays like 1/n.
  So the real collapse is much faster and much worse than the table suggests.

  THIS IS THE LOCALITY FINDING WITH TEETH:

  A flat lattice -- locally well-behaved, kappa = 0, no bottlenecks inside any
  neighbourhood -- gets PROGRESSIVELY WORSE at letting a cell escape to its
  complement as it grows, and the exact law is 4/n. A lattice grown with
  negative curvature does not have this problem.

  The fleet builds STRUCTURED lattices. That is the losing side of the axis,
  and the trade-off ledger's 'Locality: optimised by 0, taxed by 2' is worse
  than an accounting error -- it is a default that gets monotonically more
  expensive with scale.

  WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY: it does not say random growth is the answer. Random
  curvature is negative, and negative curvature is the regime Hutchcroft-Munch
  measure as near-diffusive, which is local in time but not in space. Trading
  4/n for O(1) conductance by abandoning structure is a real trade, not a win.

  HONEST CAVEATS:
   * cond is exact only for n<=24; above that it is a bound, and the bound is
     loose by ~30x at n=196. The DIRECTION is trustworthy; the MAGNITUDE is not.
   * kappa is exact only for n<=70 (O(n*deg) LPs). Large-n rows have none, and
     curvature is the thing a reader most wants.
   * the exact 4/n is the checkable anchor; the measured column should be
     compared against it and is NOT within an order of magnitude at large n.
     That gap is reported, not hidden.
   * this measures a TREND across families. It is not a proof and not a
     refutation of anything in the literature -- it is a picture of the setting
